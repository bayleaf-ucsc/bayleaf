# BLUGs: BayLeaf Users' Groups

*Version 0.1. This is a living document. It will change as the first BLUGs
learn what works. If you run a BLUG, please improve this page.*

## Why BLUGs?

In 1955, a group of companies running IBM's early computers formed
[SHARE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHARE_(computing)). They pooled their
software, compared notes and sent IBM formal lists of what they needed. IBM
listened, because those users together had real weight. For decades after
that, [users' groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Users%27_group) were one
of the main ways people learned powerful, fast-changing technology and had a
say in where it went.

AI tools are powerful and fast-changing too, but their users mostly don't
have groups. There are subreddits and hashtags, which are good for tips but
anonymous, scattered and without any shared voice. The major AI companies
learn from usage data, not from organized users. Individual users have
almost no leverage over closed systems, and the company-run communities
that do exist are mostly there to help the product spread.

BayLeaf is different:

- **Its users share a place.** We're on the same campus, with shared stakes
  in teaching, research, work and policy.
- **It can change.** BayLeaf is open, and its maintainer reads requests. A
  group's request can turn into actual changes.
- **It's small enough that groups matter.** Five people from one program
  agreeing on what they need carries real weight here.

BLUGs aren't only about BayLeaf. Most of us also use ChatGPT, Claude and
similar tools, and BLUGs welcome all of that. The goal is a local, trusted
place to learn and push back, with BayLeaf improving as a side effect.

**The big AI companies have users. BayLeaf can have users' groups.**

## What is a BLUG?

A BLUG is a small, self-run group of people who use BayLeaf or BayLeaf-like
AI systems. Members meet to:

- **Share** what they've recently learned to do
- **Raise concerns** about privacy, academic integrity, reliability, labor,
  environmental cost or anything else
- **Brainstorm** what their community needs from these tools
- **Send requests upstream** so BayLeaf gets better

**A BLUG is not:**

- A help desk (though people will get help)
- Run by the BayLeaf maintainer
- Limited to fans. Skeptics are welcome and useful.

## Who can start one?

Anyone. You don't need to be an expert or get permission. BLUGs work best
when members share some context, so natural starting points include:

- A department, program or research lab
- A course or TA team
- A staff unit
- An interest group (e.g., writing, coding, teaching, accessibility)

## Your first meeting

**Size:** 4–10 people. Small is fine. Small is often better.
**Length:** 45–60 minutes.
**Place:** A room with a screen to share, or a video call. Snacks help.

### Suggested agenda

| Time   | Segment              | What happens |
|--------|----------------------|--------------|
| 5 min  | Welcome              | Names, plus "one way I've used (or avoided) AI tools" |
| 15 min | Show-and-tell        | 2–3 people each take ~5 minutes to show something they did with any AI tool. Small demos are great. Failures count. |
| 15 min | Concerns & friction  | What's frustrating, worrying or confusing? Write it down without judging. |
| 10 min | One request upstream | Pick one thing to ask BayLeaf for (see below). |
| 5 min  | Next time            | Choose the next host and date. |

**Tip:** Start with show-and-tell, not troubleshooting. If the meeting
opens with problems, it tends to turn into a help desk, and the people who
came to share stop showing up.

## Keeping it going

- **Rotate the host every meeting.** Groups that depend on one person end
  when that person gets busy.
- **Meet on a regular schedule** (for example, the first Thursday of each
  month), so nobody has to schedule each meeting from scratch.
- **Keep a shared notes doc** with demos, concerns and requests. It becomes
  your group's memory.
- **It's okay to be small or to pause.** A group of three that meets twice a
  quarter still counts.

## Sending ideas to BayLeaf

This is what an organized group can do that a scattered audience can't.

1. Agree as a group on a request, concern or bug.
2. Open a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/bayleaf-ucsc/bayleaf/issues/new)
   and start the title with `[BLUG: <your group name>]`. No GitHub account,
   or rather not post publicly? Use the private email route in
   [SUPPORT.md](SUPPORT.md).
3. Describe who wants it, why and what you tried. Context from a group
   carries more weight than a single report.

The maintainer may visit a BLUG as a **guest** when invited, but won't run
your group. A BLUG that would keep going without BayLeaf's maintainer is the
goal.
